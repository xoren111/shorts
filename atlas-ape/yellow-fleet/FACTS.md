# The Yellow Fleet — verified fact pack (20.10.2026)

Source of truth for the script and the build. Condensed from research/facts_research.md (full sources, confidence, 17 source
conflicts). Every spoken line and every on-screen label must come from here, in the SAFE wording. IDs are used in SCRIPT.md.

## A. War and blockade
- A1 · 5 June 1967, Six-Day War. A convoy of cargo liners had entered the canal at Suez that morning, sailing NORTH toward the
  Mediterranean (coming home from Australia / the Far East). They were ordered to anchor in the Great Bitter Lake. Egypt closed
  the canal on 6 June. Safe: «They were in the canal when a war broke out.» Never «southbound». (HILL, HAPAG-1975, MARE, WP)
- A2 · The war itself lasted six days (5–10 June 1967).
- A3 · Egypt SANK ships to block the canal (RN 1974 booklet: nine Egyptian ships + a concrete caisson; US 1974 operation removed
  ten wrecks). Mines and unexploded weapons piled up over 1967–73. Safe: «Egypt sank ships to block the canal.»
  Never «flooded», never «sank a bridge into the canal» (a railway bridge was blown up — not used).
- A4 · After the war the canal was the front line: Israel's army on the east bank, Egypt's on the west, the lake a no-man's land.
  Crews could not go ashore. (HILL, ZENITH)

## B. Ships
- B1 · 15 ships from 8 countries were trapped; 14 sat together in the Great Bitter Lake (their club badge carries «14»), the
  15th (US *Observer*) alone in Lake Timsah. Say «fourteen ships» for the lake. (HILL, TIME 1969, WP)
- B2 · Flags of the 14: UK 4 (Agapenor, Melampus, Port Invercargill, Scottish Star), West Germany 2 (Münsterland, Nordwind),
  Sweden 2 (Killara, Nippon — neutral), Poland 2 (Djakarta, Bolesław Bierut), USA 1 (African Glen), France 1 (Sindh),
  Bulgaria 1 (Vasil Levski), Czechoslovakia 1 (Lednice). West + neutral Sweden + East bloc → «East and West».
- B3 · From 1969 the ships were tied together side by side in three groups to save crew.

## C. Life on the lake
- C1 · Stuck almost eight years: 5 June 1967 → May 1975 («eight years» is acceptable rounding).
- C2 · Sandstorms kept coating them in a yellow film of desert dust → «the Yellow Fleet». Not buried, not painted.
- C3 · CREWS ROTATED: the original crews went home in summer/autumn 1967; relief crews served a few months each; about 3,000
  sailors over eight years. Safe: «The sailors took turns.» NEVER imply one crew stayed eight years.
- C4 · Friendship across the Iron Curtain is confirmed by captains who were there (SCHARRNBECK, HILL). Safe: «Sailors whose
  countries were rivals became friends.»
- C5 · Great Bitter Lake Association founded 7 Oct 1967 on the British *Melampus*: mutual help across all 14 ships; badge,
  flag, ties, an anthem to the tune of «Yellow Submarine».
- C6 · Bitter Lake Olympics, late Sept – 12 Oct 1968 (the Olympic year; they ended as Mexico City opened — NOT «at the same
  time»): 14 events — lifeboat sailing/rowing, swimming, diving, high jump, sprint, shooting, football … Poland 1st,
  West Germany 2nd, Britain 3rd (med-high). Medals: say just «medals».
- C7 · Football on the deck of the British *Port Invercargill*, inside nets so the ball would not fly into the lake. Not «the
  biggest ship».
- C8 · Movie nights on the Bulgarian *Vasil Levski* (films sent by the companies, reels swapped). A pool on the Swedish
  *Killara*. A Polish ship's doctor treated everyone. Sunday meetings on *Nordwind* (do not show drinking).
- C9 · Food swaps between ships (German eggs, Japanese frozen fish from the Swedish *Nippon*, Australian apples from the British
  ships, French prawns); a Swedish ship nicknamed «the Bitter Lake Cow» made milk.
- C10 · Fighter jets fought over the lake in 1969–70; in 1973 the war came to the lake's north end; *African Glen* was hit and
  sank in 1973. No crew member is recorded as harmed. (Not used in the main VO — see SCRIPT alternatives.)

## D. Stamps
- D1 · Crews made their own stamps (linocut, the baker's yellow ink, a sailor standing barefoot on the block, perforated with a
  sewing machine — HILL/Fischer 1972, med). 784 different stamps catalogued 1967–74. Now collectors' items.
- D2 · ⚠ NOT official Egyptian postage. Most letters needed Egyptian stamps too; a few got home with only the homemade ones.
  Safe: «Even their own stamps.» / «Some letters got home with only their homemade stamps.» Never «Egypt recognised them».

## E. Return
- E1 · When they were freed (May 1975, before the official reopening on 5 June 1975), only the two West German ships,
  *Münsterland* and *Nordwind*, could sail home under their own power; most others were towed away / scrapped; *African Glen*
  had sunk in 1973.
- E2 · 24 May 1975, Hamburg: fireboats sprayed fountains, tugs sounded sirens, «tens of thousands» cheered (30,000+ /
  nearly 100,000 in different sources). Never say «exactly 30,000».
- E3 · *Münsterland*'s whole voyage (Hamburg → Australia → Hamburg, from Feb 1967) took 8 years, 3 months and 5 days — that is
  the whole trip, not the time in the lake. «Delivered only eight years late» = joke on that trip, acceptable.
- E4 · *Münsterland* was fully laden: ~3,000 t steel and steel wire, ~2,000 t wool, >1,000 t special sand, tinned fruit, hides,
  farm machinery, «among them five tons of kitchen clocks»; 300 t pears and almost 8 million eggs in the cold stores.
  By 1975 the tins had burst, but the wool, hides, sand, lead and steel wire arrived in good shape (low humidity).
  (HAPAG-1975: Hapag-Lloyd writer, American Shipper Aug 1975, reprinted by FreightWaves — single contemporary source, med.)
  Safe: «Part of the cargo? Kitchen clocks.» Never claim the clocks still worked or were sold — their fate is not recorded.

## F. Hook & comparison
- F1 · Ever Given blocked the Suez Canal 23–29 March 2021 — six days. (WP «2021 Suez Canal obstruction»)
- F2 · A normal Suez Canal transit takes about 11–16 hours (≈ 14 h average) → «a one-day trip» is safe.
  (amnautical.com «History of the Suez Canal»; CIA World Factbook entry via global-geography.org) Main hook since v2.
  F1 is only used in the optional Ever Given ending.

## DO NOT SAY
«the crews/sailors were stuck for eight years» · «southbound» · «flooded the canal» · «sank a bridge» · «15 ships in the lake»
· «15 countries» · «all sailed home» · «freed on 5 June 1975» · «Egypt recognised/accepted their stamps» · «turned yellow /
painted / buried in sand» (say «dusted yellow») · «Olympics at the same time as Mexico» / «Tokyo» · «exactly 30,000» ·
«football on the biggest ship» · «sailors were killed» · «a communist micronation» · Norway as one of the countries.
