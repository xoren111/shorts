# Pig War 1859 — facts and safe wording (checked 22.10.2026)

Sources: Wikipedia «Pig War (1859)» (research/wiki_pig_war.txt), HistoryLink essay 5724 (Kit Oldham, after Vouri 1999,
research/historylink_5724.html), NPS San Juan Island NHP.

| # | VO line | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | America and Britain almost went to war. Over one pig. | 1859 US–UK standoff on San Juan Island, triggered by the shooting of a pig; «no human casualties on either side, only a pig» | Wiki lead |
| 2 | A farmer caught it eating his potatoes. So he shot it. | 15 Jun 1859, Lyman Cutlar (American farmer from Kentucky) found a pig rooting in his garden eating his tubers (HistoryLink: Berkshire boar in his potato patch, not the first time) and shot it | Wiki, HistoryLink |
| 3 | The farmer was American. The pig was British. | Pig owned by Charles Griffin, Irishman (British subject in 1859) running the Hudson's Bay Company sheep ranch (Belle Vue Farm) | Wiki |
| 4 | He offered ten dollars. The owner wanted a hundred. | Cutlar offered $10, Griffin demanded $100 (HistoryLink: Griffin said $100, Cutlar said not worth $10) | Wiki |
| 5 | Both countries claimed the island. | Oregon Treaty 1846: border «through the middle of the channel» — Haro Strait (US claim, west of the islands) or Rosario Strait (British claim, east) | Wiki |
| 6 | America sent over four hundred soldiers. Britain sent five warships. | first 66 soldiers (Pickett, 9th Infantry), by 10 Aug 1859: 461 Americans with 14 cannons vs 5 Royal Navy warships, 70 guns, 2,140 men | Wiki |
| 7 | The governor wanted the Americans gone. The admiral said no. Not over a pig. | Governor James Douglas ordered Capt. Hornby to dislodge the Americans (avoiding armed conflict if possible); Rear Admiral Baynes would not escalate into a war «over a squabble about a pig» | Wiki |
| 8 | Both sides stayed. For twelve years. And partied together. | joint military occupation (≤100 men each), British camp north, American camp south; visited each other's camps for national holidays, athletic competitions; «next 12 years» (1860–1872) | Wiki |
| 9 | A German emperor settled it. The island went to America. | Treaty of Washington 1871 → arbitration by German Emperor Wilhelm I (3-man commission, Geneva); 21 Oct 1872 decided for the US line via Haro Strait; British left 25 Nov 1872 | Wiki |
| 10 | Total casualties: one pig. | «there were no human casualties on either side, only a pig» | Wiki lead |

DO NOT SAY: «the pig was black» (Wiki says «a pig»; HistoryLink: Berkshire boar → we show a Berkshire: black with white
snout/feet/tail tip), «Cutlar was arrested» (only threatened), «a battle», «the Kaiser drew the line himself» (commission
decided, he issued the award), «2,000 soldiers» (2,140 were men aboard the ships: sailors + marines).
On-screen extras (labels, not voice): San Juan Island · 1859; 400+ soldiers (VO «over four hundred»; 461 by 10 Aug) and 14 cannons; 70 guns · 2,140 men; Kaiser Wilhelm I; 1860 → 1872.
Bonus for the description: the Union Jack is still raised daily at English Camp by US National Park Service rangers.
