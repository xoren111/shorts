  * ALL of Australia's rabbits descend from Austin's 24 (Wikipedia: 'entirely descended'). — The PNAS study says 'the large majority of mainland Australian rabbits'. In its sample, 57 of 62 mainland rabbits fall in the main group, while rabbits from Sydney and Cattai have separate, domestic origins. Tasmania and New Zealand have their own histories.
  * Austin released '13 wild rabbits' (NMA / Australian Geographic) or 'at least 13 wild rabbits' (Wikipedia, citing Agriculture Victoria). — According to the family account quoted in PNAS, the 13 animals sent from England were only 6 wild rabbits plus 7 tame hutch rabbits, and 24 arrived in Melbourne, probably after breeding on the voyage. '13 wild' gets both the number and the wild/tame mix wrong. '24 rabbits arrived' is the best-suppor
  * The rabbits were released in October 1859 (Wikipedia, both the 'Rabbits in Australia' and 'Thomas Austin' articles). — 6 October 1859 is when the shipment left England. It arrived in Melbourne on Christmas Day 1859. The 'Thomas Austin' article cites an undergraduate biology textbook for the October date.
  * Thomas Austin was the first to bring rabbits to Australia. — Five domestic rabbits came with the First Fleet in 1788, there are more than 90 records of importations to the mainland before 1859, and feral rabbits were seen in Tasmania by 1827. Austin's shipment is the one that triggered the continent-wide invasion.
  * Austin ordered '12 grey rabbits, five hares, 72 partridges and some sparrows' from his nephew. — This appears in Wikipedia with no proper citation. The sentence sits next to a WA government reference that doesn't contain it. The only well-sourced cargo description is 'hares, partridges and 24 wild rabbits' (State Library of South Australia).
  * Australia's rabbit invasion began with 24 rabbits brought from England in 1869 (ABC News, 23 Aug 2022). — This is a typo in ABC's article (lede and URL). The year is 1859, per the PNAS paper the article reports on.
  * At their peak there were about 10 billion rabbits in Australia (Feral.org.au), and they spread at 130 km per year. — No source is given for either number. The usual estimates are about 600 million in the late 1940s (NMA; Wikipedia) and about 100 km per year (PNAS 2022).
  * Nearly all of Australia's '200 million wild rabbits' trace to Austin's shipment (CNN, Aug 2022). — The PNAS paper gives no current population figure. Its '200 million' is the yearly agricultural cost in dollars ('$200 million'). The 'all' part is also overstated.
  * The ship was 'HMS Lightning'. — Lightning was a merchant clipper of the Black Ball Line, built by Donald McKay in Boston in 1854. It was not a Royal Navy ship.

### story_gold:
  * Only 13 rabbits left England, but 24 got off the ship. According to a family memoir, the wild and tame rabbits probably bred during the 80-day voyage, so the invasion was already growing before it landed. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436340/
  * They arrived on Christmas Day 1859, 'Australia's worst Christmas present'. https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/dna-profiling-solves-australia-rabbit-plague-puzzle
  * Wild rabbits were so rare around Austin's home village that his relative could find only six, half-grown babies taken from their nests and tamed, and topped up the order with seven grey pet rabbits the villagers kept in hutches. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436340/
  * 163 years later, DNA tracked the invaders back to South West England, the corner of England where Austin was born (Baltonsborough, Somerset). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436340/
  * Most of mainland Australia's rabbits trace back to as few as five mothers (estimate: 3 to 5). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436340/
  * There were more than 90 earlier attempts to bring rabbits to the mainland, including five tame rabbits on the First Fleet in 1788. None took over. The one that did was a hunter's order for wild bunnies. https://doi.org/10.1071/ZO12129
  * The ship that carried them, the record-breaking clipper Lightning, carried the animals 'free of charge'. Ten years later (October 1869) it burned and sank in Geelong harbour, about 37 km from Barwon Park. https://vhd.heritagecouncil.vic.gov.au/shipwrecks/415
  * Three years after landing, 'Austin rabbits' numbered in the thousands. By 1865 Austin himself said he had killed 20,000 on his estate. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436340/
  * Eight years after the 24 arrived, a royal prince (Queen Victoria's son Alfred) visited Barwon Park and his party shot over 1,000 rabbits in three and a half hours (show as a number, not a hunt). https://www.nationaltrust.org.au/places/barwon-park/
  * Rabbits then spread about 100 km a year, the fastest colonisation by an introduced mammal ever recorded. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436340/
  * Biology hook: a rabbit can breed at about four months old, pregnancy takes about a month, and a doe can have up to 30 babies a year. https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/rabbit-biology-ecology-and-distribution/ ; https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/businesses/pests-weeds-and-diseases/animal-pests-diseases/pest-mammals/rabbit/

  * Austin even sent a batch of his rabbits to New Zealand in the 1860s. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436340/

### myths:
  * The Rabbit-Proof Fence is the longest fence in the world (present tense). — The claim only ever applied to Fence No. 1 on completion in 1907 ('then being the longest unbroken fence in the world', J.S. Crawford 1969). Today the WA State Barrier Fence is about 1,350 km, while the Dingo/Dog Fence in eastern Australia is 5,614 km. Say 'was called the longest unbroken fence in t
  * The No. 1 fence 'could be seen from space'. — No primary source supports this. It appears in a blog attributed loosely to Broomhall. A wire-netting fence is not visible from orbit with the naked eye. What satellites do show is the contrast between native bush and cleared farmland along the line, and the cloud differences studied in the Bunny Fe
  * Fence No. 3 was 160 miles (257 km) long. — Both Crawford's 1907 Rabbit Branch report and J.S. Crawford's 1969 departmental history give 171 miles (about 275 km). J.S. Crawford explicitly says 'This fence is often quoted as being 160 miles, which in actual fact is incorrect'.
  * Fence No. 1 was completed in 1903, or in 1906. — Contractors finished only the southern 465 miles by May 1904. The PWD completed the full 1,139 miles in September 1907 (20 or 30 Sept by different accounts). The 1903 date (Dumbleyung inventory) probably confuses the southern section reaching 315 miles in August 1903; 1906 appears in one inHerit rec
  * The fence ran to Wallal on Eighty Mile Beach. — Wallal is where Canning's survey ended. J.S. Crawford 1969 and Wikipedia put the fence's northern end at Cape Keraudren, the western end of Eighty Mile Beach. Crawford's 1907 report says 'about twenty miles North of Condon'.
  * It cost '$250 per kilometre'. — The contemporary figure is £167 1s per mile (PWD Annual Report 1907/08, total £337,941). The '$250/km' is a later decimal-era conversion repeated online. It is not a period figure, and it does not match a simple conversion (£167/mile is about £104/km). Use pounds.
  * Exact construction force: '120 men, 350 camels, 210 horses and 41 donkeys' stated as settled fact. — This comes from Broomhall's 1991 book via a self-published camel-farm page. Other sources say 'more than 400 men' (WA Dept of Agriculture 2001) and '189 camels and 128 horses' (Wikipedia PWD). Anketell himself in 1907 said '40 camels ... 150 horses, 50 donkeys' with 80-100 men in camp. No single rel
  * Total length 3,237 km. — Slip for 3,256 km (2,023 miles). The PWD 1907/08 total is 2,023 miles. Crawford's 1907 report gives 2,050 miles.
  * Fence No. 2 was built between March and July 1904. — Construction started about 23 March 1904. Crawford's annual report for the year to 30 June 1905 is what reports it complete to Gum Creek. A 724-mile fence in four months is implausible, and the 'four months' line in J.S. Crawford's text lacks a year. SLWA says 'built in 1905'.
  * Rabbits reached Twilight Cove 'near Esperance'. — Twilight Cove is on the Great Australian Bight coast, hundreds of km east of Esperance. The SLWA wording is geographically misleading, so do not use it on a map.

### myths:
  * Within 10 years two million rabbits a year could be shot or trapped with no effect on the population. — It is repeated everywhere but has no source: the Wikipedia sentence carries a 'Citation needed' tag (Jan 2021). It may be true, but it is not verified.
  * Myxomatosis killed 99.8% of Australia's rabbits. — 99.8% is the case-fatality rate among infected rabbits, not the share of the national population killed. Field losses were 99–100% in some places, and the 1952 field team estimated 'tens of millions' killed nationally.
  * Myxomatosis cut the rabbit population from 600 million to 100 million in two years. — The Wikipedia 'Rabbits in Australia' article cites an archived CSIRO page that says 'from an estimated 600 million' but gives no 100 million figure, and the Wardang Island article cites only 'National Archives of Australia'.   * The escaped calicivirus killed 10 million rabbits within two months. — Wikipedia cites a CSIRO 2008 factsheet for this. The archived factsheet text that could be extracted says only that the virus 'killed large numbers of rabbits' and does not show this figure. It is unverified, so don't use it.
  * The 2017 national release wiped out around 60% of rabbits. — This mixes up two viruses. The official 2017 K5 release gave only about 34% local knockdown. The 60% average drop came from RHDV2, which arrived on its own around 2014–15 and was never released.

### story_gold:
  * The rules forgot birds: the £25,000 prize banned anything harmful to 'horses, sheep, camels, goats, swine, or dogs', and Pasteur entered chicken cholera, a bird disease. Nobody won the prize, but Pasteur's team then earned more than the prize money making anthrax vaccines on the same island. https://www.science.org.au/news-events/news-views/adrien-loir-louis-pasteur-australasian-rabbit-plague
  * The island lab built for the rabbit war also quarantined Sarah Bernhardt's dogs in 1891. https://dictionaryofsydney.org/blog/the_island_laboratory
  * Rabbits 1, Mongoose Army 0: at least 1,000 mongooses were released in 1883–84, and rabbit trappers killed them partly to protect their own jobs. https://doi.org/10.1071/ZO10043
  * In 1886 the NSW government shipped 400 cats to a station on the Paroo River to fight rabbits, which is ironic now that feral cats are one of Australia's worst pests. https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080559/080559-07.001.pdf
  * Near-miss: in December 1950 scientists thought myxomatosis had failed again. Farmhands at Balldale saw sick rabbits but fumigated the warrens before the team arrived. Nine weeks later the disease covered about 1,760 x 1,600 km, carried by mosquitoes after a record wet year. https://csiropedia.csiro.au/Myxomatosis-to-control-rabbits/
  * Three of Australia's top scientists, one a future Nobel laureate (Burnet, 1960) and one the man who later announced the end of smallpox (Fenner), injected themselves with enough rabbit virus to kill 100–1,000 rabbits. It started as a dare from a Mildura councillor. https://www.wehi.edu.au/about/history/myxomatosis-experiment/
  * Arms race: within seven years the same virus that killed 90% of rabbits at one site killed only 26%. By the 1990s the virus had evolved to be more lethal than the 1950 original. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4379559/
  * The great escape: calicivirus was locked in quarantine pens on an island but got out on flies within six months, three years before its planned 1998 release. Bushflies on the island had jumped about a thousandfold just before. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12558351/
  * Western Australia held an official release ceremony with six infected rabbits on 18 October 1996, five months after the virus had already arrived at its border on its own. https://www.wa.gov.au/government/media-statements/Court%20Coalition%20Government/Six-rabbits-infected-with-RCD-released-nr-GS-town-of-Cranbrook-19961018
  * The uninvited guest won: RHDV2, which nobody released, cut rabbits by about 60%, while the officially released K5 managed only about 34% and was outcompeted. https://pestsmart.org.au/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/05/Australias-Rabbit-Biocontrol-Pipeline-Strategy.pdf
  * Today there are again an estimated 200 million rabbits, and scientists say a new virus is needed every 10–15 years. The match is not over. https://www.queenslandcountrylife.com.au/story/9316922/agriculture-minister-julie-collins-unveils-urgent-rabbit-strategy/
