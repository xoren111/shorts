# Australia vs Rabbits — verified fact pack (19.10.2026)

Source of truth for the script. Built from research/research.json (6 researchers + 5 independent adversarial checkers).
Every spoken or on-screen number must come from this file, in the SAFE wording. IDs are referenced in SCRIPT.md.

## A. Origin (1859)
- A1 · 24 rabbits ARRIVED in Melbourne on Christmas Day 1859 on the clipper ship *Lightning*, for Thomas Austin's estate
  Barwon Park (near Winchelsea, ~37 km from Geelong, Victoria). Say «arrived», not «he released 24». (PNAS 2022 Alves et al.;
  Cambridge; State Library SA; WA DPIRD)
- A2 · Motive: game for hunting / his shooting parties. (National Trust; PNAS)
- A3 · Family records say only 13 rabbits left England (6 hand-tamed wild babies + 7 grey hutch rabbits); 24 arrived → they
  PROBABLY bred during the ~80-day voyage (6 Oct → 25 Dec 1859). Hedge: «family records say», «probably». (PNAS quoting
  Joan Palmer's family memoir)
- A4 · It was not the first time rabbits came: 5 tame rabbits on the First Fleet (1788), >90 earlier records on the mainland —
  none became an invasion. Austin's shipment did. (PNAS; Peacock & Abbott 2013)
- A5 · 2022 DNA study (PNAS): «the large majority» of mainland rabbits trace back to this one 1859 shipment, and to
  South-West England (Austin's home region, Somerset). Say «most», never «all».
- A6 · Within three years: thousands. By the mid-1860s >10,000 shot on Austin's estate alone (tallies differ: 14,000 / 20,000).
  Safe: «more than ten thousand». (PNAS; NMA)
- A7 · Biology: breed at ~4 months old; pregnancy ~1 month (28–30 days); in good conditions a female can have up to 30 babies a
  year. (DPIRD; PestSmart) · «one pair → 184 in 18 months under ideal conditions» (Agriculture Victoria, attributed).

## B. Spread & scale
- B1 · Spread up to ~100 km a year in open country (10–15 km/yr in wet forest). Scientists call it the fastest spread of an
  introduced mammal ever recorded (attribute: «scientists say»). (PNAS citing CSIRO Stodart & Parer 1988)
- B2 · Dated front: Murray River → NSW by 1880; Queensland by 1886; Eucla on the WA border 1894; WA west coast by the 1920s.
  (NMA; King 1990) Never «1906».
- B3 · Within ~70 years they covered ~70 % of Australia (~5.3 million km²). (CSIRO/CISS) Never «the whole continent in 50 years».
- B4 · Peak: an estimated 600 million by about 1950 (CSIRO). «Estimated». ≈ «more than 70 for every Australian» (our arithmetic,
  late 1940s).
- B5 · Today: an estimated ~200 million (rough best guess; NMA «at least 150 million»).
- B6 · Damage today: ~A$200 million a year to agriculture (DAFF 2025: $197m → say «around 200 million dollars»).
- B7 · Rabbits threaten 322 of Australia's nationally listed threatened species — more than any other invasive species
  (Kearney et al. 2018). Say «more than 300 threatened species» (most are plants).

## C. The fence (Western Australia)
- C1 · 1901 Royal Commission → build a barrier fence. Work began December 1901 (Burracoppin).
- C2 · Rabbit-Proof Fence No. 1: ~1,830 km (1,139 miles), from the Southern Ocean (Starvation Boat Harbour) to the Indian Ocean
  (Cape Keraudren) — «from one ocean to the other», north–south INSIDE Western Australia (not «across Australia east–west»).
  Finished September 1907. On completion it was CALLED «the longest unbroken fence in the world» (past tense, attributed).
- C3 · All three WA fences together: ~3,256 km (2,023 miles); official cost £337,941.
- C4 · THE IRONY: by 1902 rabbits had already been found west of the planned line — five years before the fence was finished.
  (State Library WA; NMA) Say «already on the other side / on the safe side», «before it was finished».
- C5 · 1904: the inspector found rabbits 15–20 miles on the «safe» side, and tracks where they were scratching to get BACK east
  through the netting. (WA Dept of Agriculture reprint of 1904 report)
- C6 · So they built a second fence (1904–05) and a third (1906–07).
- C7 · 1907: chief inspector Alex Crawford was «thoroughly convinced» farmers inside had «practically nothing» to fear.
  By 1918 a parliamentary committee found rabbits «in thousands» inside fence No. 1.
- C8 · 1907 report: on the rabbit side «not a blade of grass», on the protected side grass 3–6 inches high (split-screen visual).
- C9 · Building it: camel teams hauled the wire up to 250 miles (400 km) inland at 10–12 miles a day; 25 boundary riders (some on
  bicycles) patrolled ~2,000 miles in 1907.
- C10 · Verdict: the fences slowed the rabbits but didn't stop them; historians call them a failure as a rabbit barrier. (NMA)
- C11 · Today the fence lives on as the State Barrier Fence (>1,300 km) — its job now: keeping out wild dogs and migrating EMUS
  (it was even renamed «the Emu Fence» for a while). (DPIRD) Don't say it «beat the emus».
- C12 · Australia-wide, at the height of the fencing craze, an estimated 320,000 km of rabbit-proof fence (NMA) ≈ 8× around the Earth
  (our arithmetic).

## D. The other weapons
- D1 · Shooting, trapping, poison, ripping warrens: kept numbers down locally, never stopped the plague.
- D2 · 1883–84: at least 1,000 mongooses released to hunt rabbits — not one population took hold. (Peacock & Abbott 2010)
- D3 · 1886: NSW sent 400 cats to a sheep station on the Paroo River to fight rabbits. (single source — Abbott 2008; optional)
- D4 · 1887: NSW offered a £25,000 prize for any new way to exterminate rabbits. Nearly 1,500 entries. Nobody ever won.
- D5 · Louis Pasteur entered — with chicken cholera (a poultry germ). His team (nephew-by-marriage Adrien Loir) got a lab on tiny
  Rodd Island in Sydney. The prize rules banned harm to horses, sheep, camels, goats, pigs, dogs — and forgot birds. Main reason
  for rejection (1889): the germ didn't spread from rabbit to rabbit. Afterwards his team made anthrax vaccine on the island
  (>4 million farm animals vaccinated 1890–94).
- D6 · Myxoma virus: first trial on Wardang Island (SA) in 1937; by 1944 judged a failure for most of Australia; 1950 trials in
  the Murray Valley again looked like a failure (77 sick rabbits among ~4,000, then nothing).
- D7 · Then the very wet 1950 summer bred clouds of mosquitoes that carried the virus: within nine weeks outbreaks stretched
  across an area about 1,760 × 1,600 km. (CSIRO)
- D8 · It killed up to 99.8 % of the rabbits it INFECTED (case fatality; say «99 percent of infected rabbits»). NOT «99 % of all
  rabbits». National drop unmeasured; NAA says 600M → ~100M in two years (disputed by peer review) → avoid the 100M figure.
- D9 · 1951: when people blamed the rabbit virus for a human brain-fever outbreak, three top scientists (Frank Fenner, Macfarlane
  Burnet, Ian Clunies Ross) injected themselves to prove it was safe for humans. None got sick.
- D10 · Arms race: at one NSW site the same test strain that killed 90 % of rabbits killed only 26 % seven years later; the virus
  also evolved. Rabbits evolved resistance.
- D11 · Rebound: an estimated ~300 million by 1995 (CSIRO; disputed — on screen «hundreds of millions»).
- D12 · Rabbit calicivirus: tested in quarantine pens on Wardang Island from March 1995, ahead of a release planned for 1998.
  Late September 1995 it ESCAPED, most likely carried by flies; by mid-October it was killing rabbits on the mainland; within
  weeks it turned up >300 km away.
- D13 · Official release only in 1996 — Western Australia's ceremony (18 Oct 1996: six infected rabbits) came after the virus had
  already spread on its own through more than 30 WA shires.
- D14 · Calicivirus cut rabbits by about 60 % nationwide, up to 95 % in the dry inland. (peer-reviewed)
- D15 · 2015: a new strain (RHDV2) turned up on its own — nobody released it — and cut numbers ~60 % on average; the 2017
  deliberate release (K5) had little clear effect and was outcompeted.
- D16 · Scientists say Australia needs a new biocontrol every 10–15 years to stay ahead.

## DO NOT SAY (myths found in competitors)
- «10 billion rabbits» — no source. «200 million from the PNAS study» — PNAS's 200 million is DOLLARS.
- «He released 24 wild rabbits» (24 arrived; mix of wild + tame) · «all rabbits descend from Austin's 24» (most).
- The quote «a few rabbits could do little harm … a touch of home» — no primary source (apocryphal).
- «Two million shot a year without effect» — Wikipedia, citation needed.
- «Fastest spread of any mammal» (only «introduced mammal», attributed) · «100 km/year everywhere».
- «Myxomatosis killed 99 % of Australia's rabbits» · «calicivirus killed 10 million in two months».
- «The fence is the longest fence in the world» (present tense) — the Dingo Fence (5,614 km) is a different fence.
- «The fence ran across the whole continent east to west».
- Anything about the 2002 film «Rabbit-Proof Fence» (Stolen Generations) — sensitive, keep out.
- «Foxes/cats were brought to eat rabbits and backfired» as the main twist — saturated and only partly true (keep at most D3).
